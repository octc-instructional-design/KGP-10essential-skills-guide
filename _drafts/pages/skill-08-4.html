8.4 Adaptability & Trust

Indicator 8.4 of Essential Skill 8: Collaboration and Teamwork

From Benchmark to Milestone

BenchmarkCollege entry

Acknowledges both strengths and limitations within a team.

→

Milestone 8.4End of two years of college

Adapts to changing project requirements and roles in a way that fosters mutual trust and respect amongst team members.

I Can Statements

  • I can adjust my role or approach when project needs change without creating disruption for the team.
  • I can respond to unexpected changes in a way that keeps the team's trust and momentum intact.
  • I can recognize when flexibility is needed and act on it professionally.

What to Look For

A student working at the Milestone level:

  • Adjusts their approach or role when project requirements shift without resistance or disengagement
  • Communicates proactively when their own limitations or circumstances may affect the team
  • Responds to role changes with professionalism rather than disruptive pushback
  • Maintains respectful working relationships with teammates even when circumstances are difficult or uncertain

Assessment Ideas by Discipline

Technical & Trades

  • In-PersonIntroduce a mid-project change (equipment unavailability, revised requirements, absent teammate) and observe how students adapt their role and maintain team function — structured observation checklist
  • Online / BlackboardAssign a scenario-based Discussion where a project requirement changes mid-stream; students respond describing how they would adapt their role and keep the team on track
  • Lab / HybridUse Peer Review at project completion that specifically asks teammates to assess adaptability — did this person adjust when needed without derailing the team?

Health Sciences

  • In-PersonDuring simulation, introduce an unexpected change (patient condition shift, staff change, new information) and assess whether students adapt their role and communication to maintain team trust
  • Online / BlackboardAssign a case scenario where care plan requirements change; students submit a Journal or Discussion response describing how they would adjust responsibilities and communicate the change to the team
  • Lab / HybridUse a structured debrief after a simulation where students reflect on a moment they had to adapt — what changed, how they responded, and what impact it had on the team

General Education & Business

  • In-PersonBuild a deliberate change into a group project (revised scope, lost team member, new constraint) and assess how students navigate it — both individually and as a team
  • Online / BlackboardUse a mid-project team check-in Discussion where students report on any changes to their role or responsibilities and how they handled them
  • HybridAssign an individual reflection Journal at project end — students describe one moment of unexpected change, how they adapted, and what they would do differently

Practical Tips for Faculty

  • Introducing a deliberate disruption into a team project is one of the most authentic ways to assess adaptability — a simple change in scope or timeline works
  • Proactive communication when things shift is a professional skill — make it explicit that students are expected to notify teammates when their circumstances change, and assess it
  • End-of-project individual reflections give you insight into adaptability that peer reviews and observation alone may miss
  • In online courses, the way students respond in group spaces when things change is directly observable — tone, timeliness, and problem-solving are all visible in the written record
